Which kind is it?

  1. 1

    20 lb grill tank you still want to use (empty or nearly empty)?

    Exchange or refill · retail cage

    Blue Rhino: Drop, Swap, and Go at a Propane Finder location (or text FIND to 75653). AmeriGas: exchange at partner retailers / kiosks, or some retailers refill; use the location finder for services at that site. Transport upright, valve closed, well-ventilated vehicle (AmeriGas transport tips). Never bring tanks inside the store (Blue Rhino how-it-works).

  2. 2

    20 lb grill tank you no longer need (recycle / dispose)?

    Blue Rhino recycle · or AmeriGas drop-off

    Blue Rhino recycling tips (updated 2026.03.16): write R or RECYCLE on the tank → any non-Vending Blue Rhino location → if an empty spot is in the display, notify a store employee; if no space, try another non-Vending location. They refurbish when possible or recycle. AmeriGas: never trash; exchanging is easiest, or drop off at most propane refill or exchange locations. Cuyahoga Recycles example: free recycle for any standard 20 lb grill tank (any brand) at Blue Rhino or AmeriGas cages — follow cage instructions. Fee for pure recycle drop (no exchange purchase): Blue Rhino says no cost to you for recycle; confirm at the store.

  3. 3

    1 lb / 14–16 oz camping or torch cylinder (Coleman-style, usually non-refillable)?

    Local solid waste / HHW · not the 20 lb cage

    Coleman FAQ: 16 oz All-Purpose cylinders are not refillable; empty cylinders are recyclable but each city/municipality sets the rule — use CylinderSafe / Coleman FAQ to find your local solid waste authority. Safety: empty cylinders may still hold fuel vapors — do not puncture. Cuyahoga Recycles: few options for ≤2 lb canisters; most scrap yards refuse; prefer appointment with the solid waste district (they discourage trash even when “empty”). Montgomery County, PA: HHW events accept mini camping cylinders only — not larger tanks at HHW. Do not assume the Blue Rhino 20 lb cage takes 1 lb cylinders — not stated on Blue Rhino recycle tips (those steps name 20 lb).

  4. 4

    Helium, oxygen, welding, medical, or other compressed-gas cylinder?

    Return to supplier · or local program

    Cuyahoga Recycles: refrigerant, welding, and medical cylinders through the company that supplied them; local gas suppliers (e.g. Airgas) may help. MoCo How Do I also lists oxygen / CO₂ / helium / acetylene size ranges at the Transfer Station Recycling Area — see the geo tires/propane page. Not invented as a national store list.

  5. 5

    Thinking trash, blue bin, scrap metal, or landfill?

    Usually no

    AmeriGas: cannot throw propane tanks in regular trash. Blue Rhino: do not take tanks to landfill/recycling without checking acceptance. Cuyahoga: never curbside recycling. Residual pressure = fire/explosion risk for workers. MoCo How Do I: pressurized tanks refused in blue bin and curbside scrap metal (geo page).

  6. 6

    Montgomery County, MD resident?

    Geo page · Transfer Station pad · not HHW

    Use tires / propane (geo) or live mocodropoff.com/tires-propane. How Do I material key 36: Recycling Area, hand to attendant upper lot near electronics; propane 1–100 lb; prefer vendor return. HHW refuses compressed gas cylinders. This national page does not invent MoCo Sunday gates or pad clocks.

Bring this / prep

  • Know the size: 20 lb grill vs ~1 lb / 14–16 oz camping vs other gas types.
  • Unwanted 20 lb recycle (Blue Rhino): write R or RECYCLE; use a non-Vending location; tell an employee if the cage has a free slot.
  • Exchange: keep tanks outside the store; upright transport; valve closed (Blue Rhino / AmeriGas).
  • 1 lb: do not puncture; check local solid waste / HHW before you drive (Coleman).
  • Prefer return-to-vendor / exchange when the tank is still usable.
  • MoCo: proof of residency and attendant hand-off live on the geo tires/propane page.

Not here / caution

  • A scraped list of Blue Rhino, AmeriGas, or Earth911 stores (use the locators).
  • Invented national exchange prices, star ratings, or “we tested” claims.
  • Assuming every HHW takes 20 lb tanks (many refuse large tanks; some take only mini camping cylinders).
  • Assuming MoCo HHW takes propane (it refuses; Transfer Station pad does).
  • Puncturing or cutting cylinders (Coleman: do not puncture; explosion risk even when “empty”).
  • Trash or curbside recycling as a default path (AmeriGas / Blue Rhino / Cuyahoga).
  • Meds / sharps pages (YMYL — not this site).
